# Phase 3: Strategy and ambiguity (Weeks 7–10, overlaps Phase 2)

**Depth:** **Own** (test strategy under ambiguity). **Time:** ~4 weeks, ~2–3 h/week alongside Phase 2.

## Goal
She turns vague, incomplete or conflicting requirements into a risk-based test strategy, a list of sharp questions, and a prioritized automation plan, then uses agents to widen and prune it. This is where her manual QA background is an advantage, so she builds on it instead of discarding it.

## What to learn

### Requirements and ambiguity
- [ ] Finding gaps: missing, contradictory, untestable and implicit requirements
- [ ] Turning gaps into questions; recording assumptions and who confirmed them
- [ ] Acceptance criteria that can be checked (Given/When/Then as a thinking tool, not necessarily a framework)
- [ ] Example mapping / "three amigos" conversations

### Risk-based strategy
- [ ] Risk = likelihood × impact; how to rank features
- [ ] What to test at which level: unit, API, UI, manual, exploratory (test pyramid / trophy)
- [ ] **What not to automate** (unstable, one-off, low value, better done manually)
- [ ] Regression suite design: smoke vs full; run frequency; cost of maintenance
- [ ] Test data strategy and environments (concept)
- [ ] Non-functional checks to consider: performance, accessibility, security basics, compatibility

### Exploratory testing with agents
- [ ] Charters and heuristics (she may already know these; formalize them)
- [ ] Agent-assisted exploration (Playwright MCP or agent-driven browser) and turning findings into repeatable tests
- [ ] Generating test ideas with an agent, then **pruning** (duplicates, low value, wrong assumptions)

## Practical projects

### Project 1: Ambiguous feature (mentor-supplied)
The mentor gives a feature description with **conflicting or missing rules** (e.g. a refund flow with unclear time limits). She delivers:
1. A list of 10–15 clarifying questions, ranked by risk.
2. A list of explicit assumptions.
3. A risk-ranked test strategy: what to test, at which level, what to automate now, later, or never.
4. A comparison with what an agent produces from the same description. She notes where the agent was wrong, shallow or overconfident, and where it added something she missed.

### Project 2: Agent-assisted exploration
Choose one demo app. Run an agent-driven exploratory session with a charter (e.g. "explore checkout with unusual inputs"). Convert 3–5 findings into either bug reports or repeatable tests. Record what the agent missed that a human would catch.

### Project 3: Automation portfolio decision
Take 30 test ideas (generated by an agent from a spec). She prunes to a plan: 10 automated now (and at which level), 5 later, 5 manual/exploratory, the rest dropped, each with a one-line reason.

## Agent exercises
- "List the ambiguities and contradictions in this spec. For each, ask a question."
- "What are the top 10 risks of this feature? Rank by likelihood and impact."
- "Given these test ideas, which duplicate each other? Which would you not automate, and why?"
- Ask the agent to **argue against** her own strategy, then decide who is right.

## Mentor drills
- **Week 7:** hidden contradiction in a spec. Does she find it?
- **Week 8:** an agent-produced test plan that looks complete but covers only happy paths. She finds the gaps.
- **Week 9:** a deadline cut ("half the time"). She re-ranks the plan and defends the cut.
- **Week 10:** stakeholder says "automate everything". She explains the cost and proposes an alternative.

## Done when
- [ ] She produced a strategy for an ambiguous feature with questions, assumptions, risks and an automation decision per item
- [ ] She can defend what is **not** automated and why
- [ ] She compared her strategy with the agent's and documented the differences
- [ ] She turned at least 3 exploratory findings into tests or bug reports

## Pitfalls
- Accepting a long, tidy list from the agent as a strategy. A strategy has priorities and trade-offs.
- Automating at the UI level by default. Prefer the cheapest level that can detect the risk.
- Treating assumptions as facts. Always record them and who must confirm them.
- Throwing away exploratory instincts. They are the part agents are worst at.
